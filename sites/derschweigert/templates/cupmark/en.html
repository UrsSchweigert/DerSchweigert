The app

A brewing and tasting log, effortlessly.

Cupmark is an iPhone app (iOS 17 or later, English and German) for people who care more about the bean than the cup around it.

  • Scan. Read the barcode on the bag. Known beans fill in by themselves; unknown ones you describe from pick lists.
  • Brew. The built-in timer holds method, dose, grind and water: chosen, stepped, stopped.
  • Rate. Eight fixed criteria and a closed vocabulary of flavour notes. That keeps every rating comparable.
  • Repeat. Brew a recipe again with one tap. Your history, recipes and own statistics are free.
  • Without a connection. Scan, brew and rate offline; it syncs when you are back online. CSV export any time.

Coffee only

We keep the community on coffee: no self-staging, no feed, nothing to moderate. What is stored is what you brewed and how you rated it.

Welcome by default

Your ratings count towards the overall score – unless you would rather they did not. You can switch that off at any time.

Only averages are shown. Your shelf, your cups and your notes stay with you.

Roasters

Beans from people you can meet.

Specialty means someone decided which bean gets roasted, for how long and for whom. Cupmark introduces those people – small roasteries near you, curated rather than arbitrary, with no ad slots and no paid ranking.

  • Curated, not arbitrary. A place goes to roasteries that roast their own beans and disclose origin, process and roast – no paid slots, no order by budget.
  • Straight from the roaster. Every bean links to the roastery itself, not to a middleman. You buy where it is roasted.
  • Feedback that arrives. Roasteries see how their beans are actually brewed and rated – as an average, never as an individual profile.

In the making: we are talking to the first roasteries now. As soon as one is on board it appears here, with its beans and its profile.

Community

What many people taste is more honest than an opinion.

Cupmark does not collect stars or comments. It collects structured brews: method, grind, ratio, water, eight criteria. Once five different people have rated a bean, that becomes a profile for it – a picture of how it really tastes and how it best comes out.

Cupmark does not want to be another social network: no likes, no followers, no comments, no timeline. What remains are beans, brews and what many people make of them.

The average, not the loudest voice

Only what at least five different people have experienced is shown. No single log, no stage for the strongest opinion.

Give and take

Rate a bean yourself and you see its community average. The map grows with every cup – no ads, no incentive to shout.

Your log stays yours

Only the average is shown. Your ratings feed it by default; whether they keep doing so is your call, and you can switch it off at any time.

  • You scan and rate. The bag you were going to drink anyway – logged once, in under a minute.
  • We rebuild every night. From five people the average appears; it is at most a day old and never built from a single cup.
  • Everyone sees the profile, nobody sees your log. No names, no order, no leaderboard of heavy drinkers.
In the shop

First see what others taste. Then buy.

In front of the shelf, the bag in your hand: one scan shows whether the community already knows this bean – its average, the method that suits it, proven recipes. No stars, no ad copy, just brews by people who actually made them.

  • Scan. Read the barcode and see whether this bean has data yet – right there in the shop.
  • Compare. The community average, the methods that suit it and the recipes others have repeated.
  • Decide. Buy it because it fits – or leave it and put the money into a bean that fits your taste.

What you see

The bean's profile: how it scores overall, which method gets the best out of it and which recipes others have repeated.

What you do not see

Anybody's log. No names, no comments, no paid placement – and no number where there are no real ratings yet.

FAQ

Short answers.

What people ask most – the answers cover the test and the start-up phase.

What does Cupmark cost?

Nothing during the start-up phase. Whoever joins within the first six months after the test starts keeps free access for good. A paid tier is planned afterwards; its price and terms are not settled yet and will be announced here before it applies.

Do I need an account?

Not for viewing published averages. For your shelf, brews, ratings and recipes you sign in with Apple; that creates only a pseudonymous identifier, with no name and no email address.

Where do the averages come from?

From structured brews: method, grind, ratio, water and eight criteria. A profile appears only once at least five different people have rated a bean; it is rebuilt every night and is at most a day old.

Do I decide whether my ratings count?

Yes. By default all your rated brews count, including earlier ones. If you would rather not, switch it off in the app; your ratings then leave the averages at the next successful nightly update – your own log stays unchanged.

What happens to my photos?

A label photo goes to our server so we can confirm a bean for the catalogue. All metadata is removed, the original is deleted after about two days, and the photo is never shown to others. Brew photos stay on your device.

Does Cupmark work offline?

Yes. Scanning, brewing and rating work without a connection; everything syncs when you are back online. You can export your data as CSV at any time.

Is Cupmark a social network?

No. No likes, no followers, no comments, no timeline. What remains are beans, brews and what many people make of them.

I run a roastery – how do I get listed?

Write to info@derschweigert.de. A place goes to roasteries that roast their own beans and disclose origin, process and roast. There are no paid slots.

What happens if I delete my account?

Your sign-in record, entries, ratings and profile are removed immediately, as is every version of your label photos. Copies in backups can remain for up to 35 days or about 13 months and are removed again on any restore. Your ratings leave the averages at the next nightly update.

Privacy

Privacy policy

Who is responsible

What the app does not do

No advertising, no tracking, no analytics, no crash reporting and no third-party software development kits for any of these. The app asks for no name, no email address, no location and no contacts.

What is stored, and why

  • Your account. You sign in with Apple. The app receives only a pseudonymous account identifier – no name and no email address. It is kept in an Amazon Cognito user record so that we know which data is yours (contract, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • Your log. Beans, brews, ratings, recipes and your settings (your language and whether your ratings feed the averages), kept so the app works and syncs between your devices (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • Label photos. If you photograph a bag label, the photo is sent to our server and may be checked by us to confirm a bean for the shared catalogue. It is never shown to other users. The server removes all metadata, including any location, and keeps only the cleaned copy; the original is deleted automatically within about two days.
  • Your setting for averages. Your ratings feed the community averages by default; the setting is stored with your profile. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in reliable averages for all users (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). You can object at any time, in the app or by writing to us: your ratings then stop counting, including earlier ones, and they are removed from the community numbers at the next successful nightly update. Your own log is not changed.
  • Bean look-ups. When you scan a barcode, our server looks the number up in the Open Food Facts database and may keep the product's name, brand and image in the shared catalogue. The look-up is made by our server, not by your phone, so Open Food Facts does not see your device.
  • Technical logs. The servers keep the technical records that running a service needs (for example request errors) for two weeks, only to keep the service secure and working (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Looking without an account

Viewing published averages needs no account, and without an account no user profile is created. For that request your device sends the barcode and the technical connection data to our server; the two-week technical log period applies to it.

Community averages

Averages are shown only when at least five different people have rated a bean. No individual log, rating or profile is shown to anyone else. Averages are rebuilt every night, so they are usually up to a day old; if the nightly update fails for more than three days, no averages are shown. When you delete a brew or your account, its ratings are removed from the averages at the next successful nightly update. Published averages are visible to everyone – in the app and on this page. Roasteries see only these published values, never an individual log.

Where it is processed

The data is stored with Amazon Web Services in Frankfurt, Germany (region eu-central-1). Amazon Web Services EMEA SARL is our processor. Sign in with Apple is provided by Apple, which handles the sign-in as a separate controller under its own privacy policy. We do not send app data to any other recipient, and we do not move app data out of the EU ourselves.

How long it is kept, and deleting it

Your data is kept while your account exists. In the app, Settings has a button to delete your account. It removes your sign-in account record, shelf entries, brews, ratings, recipes and profile, and every version of the label photos you uploaded, straight away. Photos of beans that were already verified stay in the shared catalogue for 90 days after the verification (up to about four months counting older versions), but they are no longer linked to you.

Backups: after a deletion, copies can remain in the database's point-in-time recovery for up to 35 days and in weekly exports for up to about 13 months. A deletion log makes any restore from a backup remove deleted accounts again before the data is used. Each entry holds only the account identifier, the identifiers of its label photos and the time of deletion; entries are deleted after 400 days (up to about 14 months counting older versions). Short-lived technical records, such as daily usage counters and unfinished uploads, expire by themselves.

Your rights

You can ask for access to your data, correction, erasure, restriction of processing, a copy in a common format and you can object to processing based on legitimate interests. The app already offers a CSV export and account deletion; for anything else write to the address above. You may withdraw consent at any time. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority, in particular in the country where you live or work; the authority responsible for us is the Bavarian State Office for Data Protection Supervision (BayLDA), Promenade 18, 91522 Ansbach, Germany.

Camera

The app uses the camera only to read a barcode, which happens on your device, and, if you choose, to photograph a label or a brew. Only a label photo you choose to take is uploaded. Brew photos stay on the device. If you turn on "Include brew photos in the device backup" in Settings, Apple includes them in the backup of that phone; that backup is Apple's service under Apple's terms, and Cupmark does not receive the photos. The setting is off by default.

Catalogue data

Product data and images come from Open Food Facts and are available under the Open Database License (ODbL): openfoodfacts.org/legal. We keep only the details needed to identify a bean.
